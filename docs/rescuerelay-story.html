RescueRelay · A guided product story

The rescue that almost did not happen.

A volunteer cancels. Food is waiting. A pantry is closing soon. Follow the people, decisions and AI tools that can turn that disruption into a reviewed recovery plan.

Prepared for the RescueRelay team project · 7 November 2026 · Author: Krishna Kallamadi

Part one · An ordinary afternoon

One cancellation starts the relay.

1

Priya prepares the rescue

Imagine Priya, a food-rescue coordinator. Harbor Market has 40 kilograms of sealed pantry goods to donate. Maple Pantry can receive them during its opening window. Priya records the donor, donation, recipient, pickup window and delivery window. She checks the recipient’s accepted categories and receiving capacity, then creates the rescue.

She works in a shared operational record: volunteers have capacity, training, availability and location information; recipients have receiving constraints. The donor is a record Priya manages, rather than a fourth login role.

2

The first volunteer cancels

Before pickup, the assigned volunteer becomes unavailable and records a cancellation reason. The rescue moves into recovery. On Priya’s pipeline, it appears in Recovery first, where the disruption is visible alongside the organization’s other rescues.

She does not simply choose the closest name. A nearby volunteer might have a vehicle that is too small, be unavailable during pickup, lack required training, or arrive after the pantry closes.

3

The planner investigates

Priya requests a recovery proposal. The default planner works without an API key. It retrieves relevant local policy passages, checks candidate eligibility and evaluates the route estimate against the pickup and delivery windows.

Suppose Alex is nearby but can carry only 25 kilograms. Jordan has sufficient capacity and training, and can fit this pickup into the relevant windows. Alex is rejected with an explanation; Jordan may be eligible. The example numbers illustrate the decision, rather than a stored demo result.

When several rescues need help, the matching algorithm considers them together. It avoids using a scarce eligible volunteer on an easy job if that leaves another rescue without a feasible match. It does not claim globally optimal routing, and its travel estimates are synthetic or distance-based, not live traffic.

4

A proposal waits for a person

The result is an explained proposal with candidate evidence and policy citations. Priya reviews it. A ready, current proposal can appear in Awaiting approval. The model or planner has not yet assigned anybody.

When Priya explicitly approves, the API checks the latest state, expiry and constraints again. The proposal expires after ten minutes; an intervening organization change makes its evidence stale. If circumstances changed, she must refresh and review a new proposal. A repeated identical approval request is protected by an idempotency key.

5

The volunteer takes responsibility

The approved assignment moves to Awaiting acceptance. Jordan signs in as a volunteer and sees the assigned rescue and relevant pickup and recipient information. Jordan accepts before recording pickup. Acceptance and pickup are separate steps, so an assignment alone never means the food is already moving.

After pickup, the record advances to the transport stage. Jordan later records delivery. These are explicit status updates, not GPS tracking. Notifications generated by the app remain in its activity/outbox views; no SMS, email or push delivery is configured.

6

The pantry closes the loop

Sam, Maple Pantry’s recipient user, sees deliveries for that recipient and acknowledges the delivered rescue. Volunteer delivery and recipient acknowledgment remain distinct facts. Priya can review the audit trail and impact figures derived from saved records.

If nobody qualifies, RescueRelay escalates instead of relaxing constraints. If a disruption occurs after pickup, it raises a custody issue rather than automatically handing the job to another driver. The coordinator must establish a real disposition before resolving the escalation.

The product’s central idea: make the next feasible action understandable, keep the evidence current, and leave the consequential decision with a person.
Part two · Who can do what?

Three logins, different responsibilities.

Coordinator

Owns organization-wide logistics: donors, recipients, volunteers, food offers, rescues, recovery proposals and approvals. Reviews policies, activity, exports and impact. Creates linked volunteer and recipient accounts.

AI access: Workspace → AI Studio → Tools & Controls. All optional AI operations require coordinator authentication.

Volunteer

Sees assigned rescues and the related donation, donor and recipient records. Can accept, record pickup and delivery, or cancel an assigned rescue. Can update their own availability and location.

Cannot browse the coordinator’s plans, organization-wide audit trail or other volunteers’ unrelated work.

Recipient

Sees rescues for their receiving location, acknowledges delivered rescues, and manages their own permitted receiving fields such as capacity, opening window and accepted categories.

Cannot inspect other recipients’ deliveries or use the coordinator’s AI tools.

A newly registered coordinator gets an isolated, empty organization. Demo roles use a separate synthetic organization. Role filtering is enforced by the API as well as the interface. There is no separate donor, super-admin or public beneficiary login in this version.

Part four · An optional AI-assisted chapter

The tools help prepare the decision.

The first story works offline. Now imagine Priya deliberately enabling optional tools for a more complex investigation. This is a capability walkthrough, not a claim that the complete sequence below has already been exercised against every live service.

1

Find relevant reference material

Priya chooses Tavily to discover information on approved public domains, or explicitly chooses Keenable for a search restricted to one approved site. A result becomes an attributed reference draft. It does not silently become an operational rule, and neither provider automatically substitutes for the other.

If the useful material is an authorized PDF or text document, LlamaIndex LlamaParse can parse the upload. Upload and later job-status checks have separate consents. Priya reviews the resulting draft and its scope before approving it as reference knowledge.

2

Make approved knowledge retrievable

Priya selects approved references for a separate Pinecone indexing operation. The app creates attributed text chunks in an organization-scoped namespace. Integrated embeddings support semantic retrieval: a question phrased differently from the source can still find relevant reference text.

Local retrieval remains available and clearly labels fallback. A reference citation provides context; it cannot overrule configured capacity, category, training or time-window constraints. Index acceptance alone does not establish that a later search can retrieve the document.

3

Ask one model to investigate

Priya selects Fireworks AI, Nebius Token Factory, or OpenAI as the model provider. These are alternative investigation engines, not three mandatory steps or a hidden voting system. She might ask the selected investigator to explain which disrupted rescues have feasible replacement options.

The investigator uses allowlisted read-only tools to inspect the disclosed evidence. It has a six-turn, ten-tool-call budget. Pinecone retrieval can be included with consent. The server’s deterministic checks and matching logic still control feasibility, and the result remains a proposal awaiting human approval. A fluent answer cannot authorize an assignment.

4

Remember presentation preferences, inspect the process

Mem0 can save, recall and forget limited preferences such as concise explanations or compact interface density, scoped to the person and organization. It is not allowed to remember operational facts such as who is available, what a vehicle can carry or whether food is safe.

With separately disclosed tracing, LangSmith can receive redacted investigation counts and tool metadata. It helps examine the process; it is not an agent that dispatches food. A trace failure does not invalidate an otherwise valid planning result.

5

A future audio shortcut

The implemented ElevenLabs adapter can transcribe an uploaded recording into a rescue-note draft. Priya would then complete the actual donor, category, quantity, sealed status and pickup window through validated fields. It is speech-to-text, not automated phone calls, speech generation or dispatch. The local ElevenLabs setup is currently parked and has not been live-tested.

Three separate decisions: consent to transfer data to an external service; approve a returned reference or donation draft; approve a recovery assignment. Opening AI Studio does not make a vendor call. Keys stay on the server, providers default off, and external results are treated as untrusted evidence.

Part five · Under the hood

A small application with explicit boundaries.

People and clientsReact 19 + Vite responsive web/PWA, with Framer Motion and Lucide icons. A separate Expo 54 + React Native 0.81 client targets Android and iOS. The web and native clients share typed contracts.
The application boundaryTypeScript, Node.js 24 and Fastify 5 handle authentication, organization and role checks, input validation, versioned actions, one-use AI consent and operation receipts. Zod supports validation; CORS and rate-limit plugins support the API boundary.
Two investigation pathsThe offline planner retrieves local policy text and checks hard constraints, then uses maximum bipartite matching. The optional model path uses a bounded tool loop and one selected external provider. Both lead to a separately reviewed proposal.
Knowledge and external adaptersPinecone retrieval/indexing; Tavily or Keenable discovery; LlamaParse document ingestion; Mem0 preferences; LangSmith tracing; ElevenLabs uploaded-audio transcription.
Durable recordsNode’s SQLite integration persists versioned organization state atomically with WAL transactions. Separate records hold users, hashed sessions, schema version and idempotent approval responses. Native sessions use OS SecureStore; web sessions stay in memory.

The core workflow is application-owned. Connecting LangSmith does not mean the app is built on LangChain or LangGraph; those frameworks are not required by the API package. LlamaParse supplies document parsing rather than replacing the planner with a LlamaIndex orchestration framework. Pinecone’s integrated embedding model supplies the configured remote text embedding capability.

Retrieval-augmented investigation

The model can use relevant local or approved remote reference passages to ground an explanation. Sources remain attributed. The offline path uses lexical and phrase matching; the configured Pinecone path adds embedding-based retrieval. Neither is permission to edit operational constraints.

Client capability differences

Web AI Studio includes uploads, references, preferences and receipts. Native AI Assist includes capability status, model selection, optional retrieval/tracing, consent, investigation, receipts and separate recovery approval. Native bundle export is not a signed store release or a physical-device test.

Part eight · Tell it in a presentation

A clear walkthrough for your audience.

  1. Start with the disruption. Explain that a scheduled pickup can fail even when a donor, recipient and willing volunteers already exist.
  2. Open the coordinator demo. Show the pipeline and Harbor Market’s recovery case. Generate an offline proposal and explain an accepted and rejected candidate.
  3. Make approval visible. Show that evidence, feasibility and a human decision precede assignment. Explain stale proposals and escalation.
  4. Show the other roles. The fixed volunteer demo is Maya, not any arbitrary replacement. Use Maya’s separate pantry rescue to show acceptance, pickup and delivery; use the recipient demo for Maple’s deliveries and acknowledgment. Do not imply these are necessarily one continuous seeded rescue.
  5. Open coordinator AI Studio. Explain each tool’s role using the optional story. Demonstrate actual operations only within the configured provider’s consent and data limits.
  6. Show evidence alongside features. Use the validation table to distinguish eight hosted live-operation passes, the local-only OpenAI check, and parked ElevenLabs.
  7. Close with the boundary. RescueRelay demonstrates explainable recovery and controlled AI integration. It still needs the listed live-workflow tests and production work before operational deployment.

A closing line you can use: “RescueRelay helps a coordinator recover a disrupted food pickup by combining shared records, explicit constraints, evidence and human approval. Optional AI makes investigation and reference preparation richer; people still own the rescue.”
